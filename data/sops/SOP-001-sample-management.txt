SOP-001: Sample Management
Version: 3.2 | Effective Date: 2025-01-15 | Review Date: 2027-01-15
Department: Quality Control | Author: QA Manager

================================================================================
SECTION 1: PURPOSE
================================================================================
This Standard Operating Procedure defines the requirements for the collection,
labeling, handling, storage, transfer, and disposal of all samples within the
facility. The purpose is to ensure sample integrity, maintain chain of custody,
and prevent mix-ups, contamination, or loss of samples that could compromise
product quality or patient safety.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: SCOPE
================================================================================
This SOP applies to all personnel who collect, handle, transport, or store
samples including: in-process samples, finished product samples, raw material
samples, environmental monitoring samples, reference standards, and retained
samples. It covers all sample types: solid, liquid, semi-solid, and gaseous.

================================================================================
SECTION 3: RESPONSIBILITIES
================================================================================
3.1 Analyst / Sampling Technician
    - Collect samples according to the approved sampling plan.
    - Label all samples immediately upon collection using the approved label format.
    - Record sample collection in the Sample Log within 30 minutes of collection.
    - Report any deviation from this SOP to the QC Supervisor immediately.

3.2 QC Supervisor
    - Review Sample Log entries daily.
    - Authorize sample disposal in the Sample Disposal Log.
    - Escalate deviations involving potential patient safety impact to QA.

3.3 Quality Assurance (QA)
    - Review and approve deviations that cannot be resolved at supervisory level.
    - Make final disposition decisions for samples involved in a deviation.
    - Authorize destruction of samples under investigation only after review.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: PROCEDURE
================================================================================
4.1 Sample Collection
    4.1.1 Verify the sampling plan is approved and current before collecting.
    4.1.2 Use only designated, cleaned, and labeled sampling equipment.
    4.1.3 Collect the volume/weight specified in the sampling plan. Do not
          under-sample or over-sample without documented justification.
    4.1.4 Collect duplicate samples when required by the testing protocol.
    4.1.5 Record the date, time, location, batch number, and collector ID at
          the point of collection. Do not backfill records from memory.

4.2 Sample Labeling
    4.2.1 Apply a pre-printed label immediately upon collection — never use
          handwritten labels as the primary identifier.
    4.2.2 Required label fields: Sample ID (unique), Material Name, Batch/Lot
          Number, Collection Date and Time, Collector ID, Storage Condition,
          Expiry Date (where applicable).
    4.2.3 If a label error is discovered post-collection, quarantine the sample
          immediately and notify the QC Supervisor before any testing proceeds.
    4.2.4 Never overwrite, deface, or remove a sample label without QA approval.

4.3 Sample Storage
    4.3.1 Store samples in the designated area immediately after collection.
          Do not leave samples unattended at room temperature unless the
          storage condition explicitly permits it.
    4.3.2 Temperature-sensitive samples must be stored within the specified
          range: Refrigerated (2–8 °C), Frozen (≤ -20 °C), or Controlled
          Room Temperature (15–25 °C). Verify storage temperature at transfer.
    4.3.3 Log refrigerator/freezer temperature twice daily. If a temperature
          excursion is detected, quarantine affected samples and notify QC
          Supervisor and QA within 1 hour of discovery.
    4.3.4 Retain samples must be stored in the designated Retain Room under
          lock and key. Access is restricted to authorized personnel only.

4.4 Chain of Custody
    4.4.1 Every transfer of a sample between personnel or locations must be
          recorded in the Chain of Custody Log. Include: transferring person,
          receiving person, date, time, sample condition at transfer.
    4.4.2 A sample that cannot account for all transfers in its chain of custody
          is considered compromised and must be quarantined pending QA review.
    4.4.3 Samples transferred to external laboratories must be accompanied by
          a Chain of Custody document signed by both the sender and courier.

4.5 Sample Disposal
    4.5.1 Do not dispose of any sample until testing is complete and results
          are approved, or until QA provides written authorization.
    4.5.2 Hazardous samples must be disposed of per the Waste Management SOP
          (SOP-012). Do not dispose of hazardous samples in general waste.
    4.5.3 Record all disposals in the Sample Disposal Log, including date,
          sample ID, and authorizing signature.

================================================================================
SECTION 5: DEVIATION CRITERIA AND ESCALATION TRIGGERS
================================================================================
5.1 The following events constitute a deviation from this SOP and must be
    documented in the Deviation Report system within 24 hours of discovery:

    (a) Sample collected without a valid, approved sampling plan.
    (b) Mislabeled sample — any field on the label is incorrect or missing.
    (c) Sample stored outside of specified temperature range for any duration.
    (d) Break in chain of custody — any unrecorded transfer.
    (e) Sample tested before label error was corrected and authorized by QA.
    (f) Sample log entry completed more than 2 hours after collection.
    (g) Sample container damaged, unsealed, or showing signs of contamination.
    (h) Retained sample accessed without authorization.

5.2 Immediate Escalation to QA is required (same business day) for:

    (a) Any mislabeled sample that has already been tested and results reported.
    (b) Temperature excursion affecting sterile or biological samples.
    (c) Chain of custody break on any finished product or release sample.
    (d) Suspected or confirmed sample mix-up between two different products.
    (e) Any sample that may have been tested and results used for a batch release
        decision where labeling or custody integrity is in question.
    (f) Unauthorized access to the Retain Room.

5.3 Risk Classification Guide:
    HIGH:   Any deviation in 5.2 above; any deviation where results have
            already been used in a batch release, regulatory submission,
            or patient safety decision.
    MEDIUM: Deviation corrected before testing; temperature excursion on
            non-sterile, non-biological samples; late log entry with
            corroborating documentation available.
    LOW:    Minor procedural deviation with no impact on sample integrity,
            discovered and corrected during the same work session.

================================================================================
SECTION 6: DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================
6.1 Sample Log — completed within 30 minutes of collection.
6.2 Chain of Custody Log — completed at every transfer.
6.3 Temperature Monitoring Log — twice daily, calibrated thermometer.
6.4 Sample Disposal Log — completed at time of disposal.
6.5 Deviation Report — within 24 hours of discovery for any Section 5.1 event.
6.6 All records must use permanent ink. Corrections: single strikethrough,
    initials, date, and reason. Use of correction fluid (Tipp-Ex) is prohibited.
