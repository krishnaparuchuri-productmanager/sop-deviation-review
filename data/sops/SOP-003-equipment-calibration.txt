SOP-003: Equipment Calibration
Version: 4.1 | Effective Date: 2024-11-01 | Review Date: 2026-11-01
Department: Quality Control / Engineering | Author: Calibration Manager

================================================================================
SECTION 1: PURPOSE
================================================================================
This SOP establishes requirements for the calibration, verification, and
maintenance of all instruments and equipment used in the manufacture, testing,
and storage of pharmaceutical products. Calibrated equipment ensures that
measurements are accurate, traceable to national or international standards,
and fit for their intended purpose. Use of equipment that is out of calibration
or has an expired calibration status is strictly prohibited.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: SCOPE
================================================================================
This SOP applies to all equipment and instruments that generate data used in:
quality control testing, manufacturing process control, environmental monitoring,
storage condition monitoring, and batch release decisions. This includes but is
not limited to: balances, pH meters, HPLC systems, UV-Vis spectrophotometers,
Karl Fischer titrators, dissolution apparatus, thermometers, temperature loggers,
pressure gauges, flow meters, and pipettes.

Equipment used for non-GMP purposes (e.g., administrative computers, general
office equipment) is excluded from this SOP.

================================================================================
SECTION 3: RESPONSIBILITIES
================================================================================
3.1 Equipment Owner / Analyst
    - Check the calibration label on equipment before every use.
    - Do not use equipment with an expired, missing, or "OUT OF SERVICE" label.
    - Report out-of-tolerance or expired calibration to QC Supervisor immediately.
    - Never remove, deface, or alter a calibration label.

3.2 Calibration Technician
    - Perform calibrations per the approved Calibration Master Schedule.
    - Record all calibration results in the Calibration Log on the day performed.
    - Affix a new calibration label upon successful completion showing:
      equipment ID, calibration date, next due date, and technician initials.
    - Place an "OUT OF SERVICE" label on equipment that fails calibration.

3.3 QC Supervisor
    - Review the Calibration Master Schedule monthly to identify upcoming dues.
    - Escalate any out-of-tolerance finding that may affect in-use data to QA.

3.4 Quality Assurance (QA)
    - Perform retrospective impact assessment when out-of-calibration equipment
      is discovered to have been used for testing or manufacturing control.
    - Approve or reject batches that may be affected by calibration failures.
    - Authorize re-testing where data generated by out-of-calibration equipment
      is used in a batch release or regulatory submission.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: PROCEDURE
================================================================================
4.1 Calibration Master Schedule
    4.1.1 All in-scope equipment must be listed in the Calibration Master Schedule
          with a unique equipment ID, calibration frequency, and responsible
          calibration technician.
    4.1.2 Calibration frequencies are defined by: regulatory requirements,
          manufacturer recommendations, equipment criticality, and historical
          performance data. Typical frequencies: balances (6 months),
          thermometers (12 months), HPLC detectors (12 months), pH meters
          (before each use — point-of-use verification required).
    4.1.3 The schedule must be reviewed and re-approved by QA annually.

4.2 Pre-Use Calibration Check
    4.2.1 Before using any instrument, visually inspect the calibration label.
    4.2.2 Confirm: (a) label is present, (b) current date is before "Next Due"
          date, (c) label status is not "OUT OF SERVICE" or "UNDER REPAIR".
    4.2.3 For pH meters and balances: perform a point-of-use verification
          (using certified buffer solutions or reference weights respectively)
          and record the result in the instrument's daily check log.
    4.2.4 If calibration has expired, affix an "OUT OF SERVICE" label, remove
          the instrument from use, and notify the Calibration Technician.

4.3 Performing Calibration
    4.3.1 Use only certified reference standards traceable to NIST (USA), NPL
          (UK), or equivalent national metrology institute.
    4.3.2 Record traceability certificate numbers for all reference standards
          used in each calibration record.
    4.3.3 Perform calibration under the environmental conditions specified in
          the equipment's calibration procedure (typically 20 ± 2 °C).
    4.3.4 If the instrument passes calibration: affix a new calibration label,
          update the Calibration Master Schedule, and file the calibration record.
    4.3.5 If the instrument fails calibration (out-of-tolerance result):
          (a) Do not return the instrument to service.
          (b) Affix an "OUT OF SERVICE" label immediately.
          (c) Notify QC Supervisor and QA within 2 hours.
          (d) Initiate a retrospective impact assessment (see Section 4.4).

4.4 Out-of-Tolerance Retrospective Assessment
    4.4.1 Determine the date of the last successful calibration. All data
          generated between that date and the out-of-tolerance discovery
          date is considered "at risk."
    4.4.2 Identify all tests, batches, and release decisions made using
          the affected instrument during the at-risk period.
    4.4.3 Assess whether the magnitude of the calibration error would have
          materially affected the results generated. Document the assessment.
    4.4.4 If the assessment cannot rule out a material impact on product quality
          or patient safety, QA must make a batch disposition decision.
    4.4.5 Any batch released based on potentially compromised data must be
          reported to the Qualified Person (QP) immediately.

4.5 Equipment Labeling
    4.5.1 Four label types are used:
          GREEN  — "CALIBRATED": In service, within calibration period.
          YELLOW — "LIMITED USE": Calibrated for specified parameters only.
                   Permitted uses listed on label.
          RED    — "OUT OF SERVICE": Not to be used. Reason noted on label.
          ORANGE — "UNDER REPAIR": Awaiting maintenance or calibration.
    4.5.2 Only Calibration Technicians may affix, remove, or replace labels.
          Analysts may affix a "DO NOT USE" sticky note but must also notify
          the Calibration Technician on the same day.

================================================================================
SECTION 5: DEVIATION CRITERIA AND ESCALATION TRIGGERS
================================================================================
5.1 The following events constitute a deviation and must be documented within
    24 hours of discovery:

    (a) Equipment used after calibration due date (overdue calibration).
    (b) Equipment used without a calibration label.
    (c) Equipment used while labeled "OUT OF SERVICE" or "UNDER REPAIR."
    (d) Point-of-use verification failure not followed by immediate quarantine.
    (e) Calibration performed with reference standards of unknown or expired
        traceability.
    (f) Calibration record not completed on the day calibration was performed.
    (g) Calibration label removed or altered by an unauthorized person.

5.2 Immediate Escalation to QA is required for:

    (a) Out-of-tolerance calibration result on any instrument used for batch
        release testing within the last 12 months.
    (b) Use of out-of-calibration equipment for any sterility, potency, identity,
        or safety-related test.
    (c) Retrospective assessment that cannot rule out material impact on
        a released, distributed, or recalled batch.
    (d) Discovery that equipment was used while labeled "OUT OF SERVICE."
    (e) Any calibration failure involving reference standards with expired
        traceability certificates.
    (f) Multiple instruments from the same calibration batch failing simultaneously
        (potential systemic issue).

5.3 Risk Classification Guide:
    HIGH:   Out-of-calibration equipment used for sterility, potency, or identity
            testing; data used in batch release or regulatory submission; cannot
            rule out patient safety impact.
    MEDIUM: Overdue calibration discovered before testing; out-of-tolerance result
            on non-critical instrument with no current testing impact; minor
            calibration record documentation error.
    LOW:    Calibration label formatting error only; administrative scheduling
            error corrected before calibration became overdue.

================================================================================
SECTION 6: DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================
6.1 Calibration Master Schedule — reviewed monthly, re-approved annually.
6.2 Calibration Record — completed on day of calibration, filed in equipment file.
6.3 Instrument Daily Check Log — for pH meters and balances, before each use.
6.4 Out-of-Tolerance Notification — to QA within 2 hours of discovery.
6.5 Retrospective Impact Assessment Report — within 5 business days of OOT event.
6.6 Equipment File — one per instrument, containing all historical calibration
    records, maintenance history, and any deviation reports.
