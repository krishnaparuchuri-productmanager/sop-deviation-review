# SOP-DS-005: Documentation Standards — Record Keeping and Completeness

**Document ID:** SOP-DS-005
**Version:** 4.2
**Effective Date:** 2025-04-01
**Department:** Quality Assurance
**Review Cycle:** Biennial

---

## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the minimum standards for creating, completing, correcting, storing, and retaining GMP records. Accurate, complete, and contemporaneous documentation is a regulatory requirement and a fundamental principle of data integrity. Records must reflect what was actually done, when it was done, and by whom — not what was intended or recalled after the fact.

---

## 2. Scope

Applies to all GMP records generated at this facility including: batch manufacturing records, laboratory notebooks, electronic LIMS entries, calibration logs, environmental monitoring logs, training records, deviation reports, CAPA records, and any other record that supports a product quality or regulatory decision.

---

## 3. Core Documentation Principles (ALCOA+)

All records must satisfy the ALCOA+ data integrity framework:

| Principle | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Attributable** | Every entry identifies who made it and when. Never record data under another person's credentials. |
| **Legible** | All handwritten entries must be clearly readable. Illegible entries are treated as missing. |
| **Contemporaneous** | Records are completed at the time of the action. Backdating or pre-dating is strictly prohibited. |
| **Original** | The first data capture is the original. Where transcription is necessary, the original must be retained. |
| **Accurate** | Entries reflect what actually occurred, not nominal or expected values. |
| **Complete** | All fields are filled or marked "N/A" with initials. Blank fields are not acceptable. |
| **Consistent** | Dates, times, units, and identifiers are used consistently and are internally logically coherent. |
| **Enduring** | Paper records use permanent ink. Electronic records require audit trail and access controls. |
| **Available** | Records are retrievable on request by QA, the QP, or regulatory inspectors. |

---

## 4. Making Entries

Record data directly into the approved form or system at the point of activity — not from scratch paper or memory. Use the date format DD-MMM-YYYY (e.g., 07-May-2026) and the 24-hour clock (e.g., 14:32). Every entry must be signed and dated at the time it is made; a signature without a date or a date without a signature is incomplete.

---

## 5. Correcting Errors

Correct paper record errors using the **single strikethrough method**: one line through the incorrect entry (original remains legible), with the correction written adjacent, initialled, dated, and a brief reason given.

The following are **strictly prohibited** and require a formal deviation report: correction fluid (Tipp-Ex, White-Out), overwriting, erasure, or multiple lines that obscure the original. In electronic systems, use only the approved amendment function — direct database edits are prohibited.

---

## 6. Record Completeness at Submission

Supervisors review records before submitting to QA. A record is complete when all fields are filled or marked N/A, all signatures and dates are present, all attachments are included, and every in-record deviation references a deviation report number. Incomplete records are returned.

---

## 7. Record Retention

Retain records per the Document Retention Schedule: batch records for 1 year post-expiry or 5 years post-manufacture (whichever is longer); calibration records for equipment life plus 2 years; training records for employment duration plus 3 years. No record may be destroyed without written QA authorization.
