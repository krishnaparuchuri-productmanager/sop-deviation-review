# SOP-CA-004: CAPA Procedure — Corrective and Preventive Action

**Document ID:** SOP-CA-004
**Version:** 2.5
**Effective Date:** 2025-01-20
**Department:** Quality Assurance
**Review Cycle:** Biennial

---

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines requirements for the identification, planning, implementation, verification, and closure of Corrective Actions (CA) and Preventive Actions (PA) arising from deviations, investigations, audits, complaints, and trend analyses. CAPA ensures that the root causes of non-conformances are eliminated and that systemic improvements prevent recurrence across the organization.

---

## 2. Scope

Applies to all CAPA actions raised in the electronic quality management system (eQMS) by any department. Corrective Actions address confirmed non-conformances. Preventive Actions address potential non-conformances identified through trending, risk assessment, or proactive review.

---

## 3. CAPA Types

**Corrective Action (CA)**
An action taken to eliminate the root cause of a detected non-conformity and prevent recurrence. A CA must be directly linked to an identified root cause from a completed investigation (SOP-IG-003).

**Preventive Action (PA)**
An action taken to eliminate the cause of a potential non-conformity before it occurs. PAs may arise from trend analysis, risk assessments, regulatory intelligence, or audit observations without a specific deviation having occurred.

---

## 4. CAPA Planning

### 4.1 CAPA Action Statement
Each CAPA action must be described in specific, measurable terms. Vague actions such as "retrain staff" or "remind operators to follow the SOP" are not acceptable without additional specificity about what will be trained, how comprehension will be verified, and what systemic change will prevent the failure mode from recurring.

### 4.2 CAPA Owner and Due Date
Assign to a named individual (not a department) with a risk-based due date: Critical CAPAs within 30 days; Major within 60 days; Minor or preventive within 90 days.

### 4.3 CAPA Effectiveness Criteria
Define measurable effectiveness criteria before opening the CAPA — the specific, observable outcome that proves the action worked (e.g., zero recurrence in 6 months; post-training assessment score ≥80%; updated SOP approved).

---

## 5. CAPA Implementation

The CAPA owner executes the action by the agreed due date and documents objective evidence of completion in the eQMS (e.g., signed training records, revised SOP version numbers, equipment qualification reports). If the due date cannot be met, request an extension from QA at least 5 business days in advance with written justification.

---

## 6. Effectiveness Verification

QA verifies each CAPA using the criteria defined at planning, after sufficient time has elapsed. Acceptable verification methods include: recurrence monitoring, re-audit of the affected area, batch record review, or repeat process qualification. If a CAPA is found to be ineffective, a new CAPA must be initiated and linked to the original. Repeated failure on the same root cause is escalated to the QA Director.

---

## 7. CAPA Closure

A CAPA may only be closed when all actions are implemented with documented evidence, effectiveness is verified, and the QA Manager has signed off. Closure without verified effectiveness is not permitted under any circumstances.

---

## 8. CAPA Trending

QA reviews all open and recently closed CAPAs monthly in the Quality Management Review. Trends by department, deviation type, and recurrence rate are reported to site management quarterly.
