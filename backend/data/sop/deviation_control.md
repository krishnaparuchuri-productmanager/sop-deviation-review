# SOP-DC-001: Deviation Control — Classification and Reporting

**Document ID:** SOP-DC-001
**Version:** 2.1
**Effective Date:** 2025-01-10
**Department:** Quality Assurance
**Review Cycle:** Biennial

---

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines the system for identifying, classifying, reporting, and tracking deviations from approved procedures, specifications, and regulatory requirements in pharmaceutical manufacturing and quality control operations. A consistent classification framework ensures that responses are proportionate to risk and that regulatory obligations are met.

---

## 2. Scope

Applies to all departments involved in manufacturing, quality control, warehousing, and distribution. Covers planned and unplanned deviations from batch manufacturing records, standard operating procedures, validated processes, and approved specifications.

---

## 3. Deviation Classification

Deviations are classified at the time of initial reporting and may be reclassified following investigation.

**Critical Deviation**
A deviation that has, or could have, a direct impact on product sterility, identity, potency, purity, or patient safety. Requires immediate containment and same-day escalation to QA and the Qualified Person (QP). Examples: use of a rejected raw material in manufacturing; sterility test failure on a released batch; aseptic process breach.

**Major Deviation**
A deviation that represents a significant departure from a validated process or approved procedure, with potential but not confirmed product quality impact. Requires QA notification within 24 hours and formal root cause investigation. Examples: in-process result outside specification; environmental monitoring Action Limit exceedance; equipment used with overdue calibration.

**Minor Deviation**
A deviation with no direct or plausible indirect impact on product quality or patient safety. Requires documentation and trending but not a formal investigation. Examples: late completion of a log entry; administrative labeling error corrected before sampling; cosmetic packaging defect below threshold.

---

## 4. Reporting Requirements

4.1 Any employee who identifies a deviation must report it to their supervisor within **2 hours** of discovery.

4.2 The supervisor must open a Deviation Report in the electronic quality management system (eQMS) within **24 hours** of discovery for Critical and Major deviations, and within **5 business days** for Minor deviations.

4.3 The Deviation Report must include at minimum: description of the event, date and time of occurrence, date and time of discovery, product or process affected, classification, immediate actions taken, and name of the reporting person.

4.4 Critical deviations must be verbally communicated to QA within **1 hour** of discovery, regardless of the 24-hour documentation window.

---

## 5. Escalation Path

| Classification | Initial Notification | eQMS Entry | QP Notification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | QA within 1 hour | Within 24 hours | Same day |
| Major | Supervisor within 2 hours | Within 24 hours | Within 48 hours |
| Minor | Supervisor within 2 hours | Within 5 business days | Not required |

---

## 6. Responsibilities

- **Operator / Analyst:** Identify and report deviations promptly. Never conceal or delay reporting.
- **Supervisor:** Classify the deviation, initiate containment, open the eQMS record.
- **QA:** Review classification, confirm or reclassify, assign investigation owner.
- **QP:** Review Critical and Major deviations before batch disposition decisions.
